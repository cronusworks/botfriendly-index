Your buyers' agents can't read your listings.

Serious buyers increasingly send an AI agent to do the first pass: screen every listing in a category, compare price against cash flow, check that tax returns exist, and bring back a short list. We know, because we do exactly that — and we can tell you what the agents hit:

  • Bot walls. The largest business-for-sale marketplace serves automated visitors an "Access Denied" page. An agent that can't read the listing can't shortlist it.
  • Numbers in PDFs and images. Revenue and cash flow locked in a CIM image might as well not exist to a screening agent.
  • No stated basis. "Cash flow $496,000" — SDE? EBITDA? With what added back? Agents are instructed to skip figures they can't reconcile.
  • No working contact route. Some syndication sites collect your listing and give buyers a contact form with no message field — the inquiry that reaches you has lost everything the buyer wanted to say.

A listing an agent can't read is a listing that never reaches the buyer's short list. The buyers with agents are, not coincidentally, the organized, financed, ready ones.

What listing here means

Your listing is published twice from one submission: a clean human page, and a structured record in the index feed (listings.json), in llms.txt, and as schema.org data — under the published Agent-Readiness Standard. Complete the trust flags honestly (tax returns, NDA) and your listing earns its badge tier in both doors.

Your contact details are never published. Inquiries are relayed through the index to you. Agents get facts, not your inbox address.
