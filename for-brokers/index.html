Your listings are screened before buyers ever see them.

Serious buyers do more of their first pass up front than they used to: listings get screened and sorted — by category, price against cash flow, whether tax returns exist — before a short list ever reaches the buyer. We shop for businesses ourselves, and we can tell you what gets a listing passed over:

  • Locked doors. The largest business-for-sale marketplace blocks whole classes of legitimate visitors outright. A listing that can't be reached can't make the short list.
  • Numbers in PDFs and images. Revenue and cash flow locked in a CIM image might as well not exist at screening time.
  • No stated basis. "Cash flow $496,000" — SDE? EBITDA? With what added back? Figures that can't be reconciled get skipped.
  • No working contact route. Some syndication sites collect your listing and give buyers a contact form with no message field — the inquiry that reaches you has lost everything the buyer wanted to say.

A listing that gets passed over at screening never reaches the buyer's short list. The buyers who screen hardest are, not coincidentally, the organized, financed, ready ones.

What listing here means

Your listing is published twice from one submission: a clean page for people, and a complete, standardized record in the index data — under the published Agent-Readiness Standard. Complete the trust flags honestly (tax returns, NDA) and your listing earns its badge tier in both forms.

Your contact details are never published. Inquiries are relayed through the index to you. Inquiries reach you; your address doesn't reach the internet.

Pricing — simple on purpose

When pricing starts, it is one annual flat fee. Not per lead, not per click, not a percentage. One flat annual fee per brokerage — the way software should be sold to small firms. Brokers who list during validation will be offered founding terms before any public price exists.
